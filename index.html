PROJECT PURPOSE

Problem this dashboard addresses

Fish-sector decision-makers need a clearer view of reported fish production, hatchery supply, production systems, locations and market information to identify where follow-up questions and investment decisions may be needed.

Core question: What do available published statistics show about Rwanda’s fisheries and aquaculture production, and what additional data would help explain production gaps and opportunities?

Intended users: students, researchers, fisheries and aquaculture planners, farmer-support organisations and development partners. This is a student prototype, not an official government reporting system.

National alignment: The second National Strategy for Transformation (NST2, 2024–2029) is described as translating Vision 2050 into five-year action. Its priorities include decent and productive jobs, higher exports, and reducing stunting and malnutrition, with agriculture targeted to grow by more than 6% a year and become more market-oriented and sustainable. This dashboard supports that agenda by tracking fish output against the 2029 and 2035 national targets, showing the hatchery supply that feeds farms, and listing value-chain areas (feed, fingerlings, cold chain, processing, regional trade) linked to jobs and exports. Fish is also a protein source: the strategy’s consumption baseline is about 7.9 kg per person a year, with an ambition near 10 kg.

METHOD & DATA QUALITY

Data used and how it was handled

  • Sources: the MINAGRI Annual Report 2024/25 (quarterly harvest and the hatchery fingerling table), the National Aquaculture Strategy 2023–2035 (targets, systems, species, market assumptions), and the NISR Agriculture Household Survey 2024.
  • Handling: figures were transcribed from these documents, then labelled as reported values, strategy estimates, targets or calculated values. These kinds of evidence are not mixed or treated as interchangeable.
  • Calculations: every calculated value is simple arithmetic with the formula shown (shares of a total, year-on-year change, gap to target, pace per year). None is a forecast.
  • NISR check: the Agriculture Household Survey 2024 covers 600 villages in 30 districts and about 2.2 million agricultural households. In its public file, the main agricultural activity of households is recorded as cropping (3,296 of 3,304 valid responses) or livestock (8), with no household recording aquaculture. Fish farming is therefore not captured as a main household activity, so fish figures here rely on MINAGRI reporting and district-level fish data would need a dedicated farm register or survey module.
  • Reporting periods: calendar-year labels (2020–2025 trend) and the fiscal year 2024/25 (quarterly harvest, hatcheries) are shown separately and should be compared only after confirming period definitions.
  • Maps: the location graphic is schematic, not a GPS-accurate district map.
POTENTIAL IMPACT

How this supports decisions

  • Planners (MINAGRI, RAB): output grew by about 3,937 t a year over 2020–2025, while reaching the 2029 target would need about 6,315 t a year, so the dashboard shows the pace gap at a glance.
  • Supply planning: one hatchery (Kivu Choice, Gisagara) produced 52.4% of listed fingerlings and two districts about 82%, which raises a supply-risk question for pond and cage farmers.
  • Seasonal readiness: harvest fell 33.5% from Q3 to Q4 of FY 2024/25, and the report links this to feed cost, seed quality and imported materials, so feed and seed supply can be reviewed ahead of Q4.
  • Development partners and investors: market baselines (about 7.9 kg consumption, about 19,000 t imports) and priority value-chain areas show where investment questions arise.
  • Statistics and research: the data-gap list (no district harvest, no aquaculture households in the household survey) shows what to collect next.

Evidence boundary: these are potential uses, not measured outcomes. Validate the data and consult sector stakeholders before recommending a specific intervention.

How the dashboard is built

  • Self-contained HTML, CSS and JavaScript: no server, no login, and it opens in any modern browser on phone or computer.

  • Interactive location map with category filters and clickable hatchery cards.
  • Charts drawn directly from the reported values, in a mobile-friendly layout.

How to read the map

The overview map shows where fish-sector activity is located and where fingerling supply comes from. Marker positions are schematic.

  • Western edge, Lake Kivu (blue): Rubavu, Karongi and Rusizi are tied to Lake Kivu cage farming and capture fisheries. Kivu is the main cage-farming lake, so the western cluster marks where large-scale cage production is concentrated.
  • Lake Muhazi (blue): the second cage-farming centre, shown in the east-central part of the country.
  • Hatcheries (green): these mark where fingerlings are produced, not where fish are harvested. Gisagara (Kivu Choice) alone supplies 52.4% of the 71.66 million fingerlings reported for FY 2024/25, and the two Rwamagana hatcheries add about 30%. Together these two districts account for roughly 82% of the listed output.
  • Eastern hatcheries (Rwamagana, Kayonza, Bugesera): these sit in the Eastern Province, the main pond-farming area, so they are close to the farms they supply.
  • Rusizi hatchery: sits on the Lake Kivu shoreline, so fingerling supply and cage farming overlap in the west.
  • Rwasave, Huye (green): shown as a hatchery location only. It is outside the six-hatchery output table, so it has no output figure.
